# Study 3: does confidence x geometry beat confidence alone?

**Not reproduced on this dataset, for the one feature that could be
tested.** Multiplying the confidence score by shape complementarity
does not beat confidence alone here, by average precision or by
within-target AUROC, and the explicit interaction term adds nothing
to the logistic model either.

This is a **partial** replication and the missing half is the more
important one: their strongest reported combination uses Rosetta
interface energy, which could not be computed here at all.

Generated by `binderkit study replication`. Every number is reproducible
from that command.

## What was and was not attempted

| Their feature | Attempted | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `shape_complementarity` | yes | licence-free reimplementation, validated against published bands |
| `interface_dG_dSASA` | **no** | PyRosetta ships no Windows build (Linux and macOS wheels only); the licence itself is free for non-commercial use and needs no credentials. Section 8.6 forbids substituting another energy function and calling it a replication |

Their two reported combinations are `ipSAE_min x interface_dG/dSASA` and
`LIS x shape_complementarity`. **The first remains untested here.** Any
statement below applies to the shape-complementarity combination only.

The licence was never the obstacle: PyRosetta is free for non-commercial
use with no form, account or credentials. It ships no Windows build, which
is a different and more tractable problem.

The Sc implementation is independent of Rosetta, so it was checked
against interfaces with published values rather than against Rosetta:
on a crystallographic antibody-antigen complex it returns 0.612 against
a published band of 0.64-0.68, and on a permanent chain pairing 0.618
against 0.70-0.76. It therefore reads **low in absolute terms** and must
not be compared with published Sc thresholds. No parameter was tuned to
close that gap. Only within-target ranking is used below, which a
constant offset cannot change. See `validate_sc.py`.

## `shape_complementarity`

1153 designs across 15 targets, binder rate
27.0%.

### (a) Their test, their way: average precision

Average precision computed inside each target and averaged over
targets. AP is the measure they report, chosen because the positive
class is rare; a random ranker scores the base rate.

| Score | Mean within-target AP |
|---|---|
| random (base rate) | 0.270 |
| `shape_complementarity` alone | 0.328 |
| `ipsae_mean` | 0.582 |
| `ipsae_mean` x `shape_complementarity` | 0.521 |

Difference -0.061, 95% CI -0.108 to -0.017, sign held in 100% of replicates: **hurts**.

Precision@k, within target, averaged over the targets with at least
k scored designs. **k = 20 is this competition's actual decision.**

| k | targets | random | confidence | confidence x feature | difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 15 | 0.270 | 0.467 | 0.447 | -0.020 |
| 20 | 15 | 0.270 | 0.433 | 0.400 | -0.033 |
| 50 | 14 | 0.270 | 0.360 | 0.344 | -0.016 |

### (b) Their test, our way: within-target AUROC

| Score | Mean within-target AUROC |
|---|---|
| `shape_complementarity` alone | 0.528 |
| `ipsae_mean` | 0.746 |
| `ipsae_mean` x `shape_complementarity` | 0.698 |

Difference -0.048, 95% CI -0.091 to -0.008, sign held 99%: **hurts**.

(a) and (b) point the same way, so the choice between AP and AUROC
is not what decides this result.

### (c) Our test, their feature: linear term, then the interaction

Leave-one-target-out logistic regression, identical to study 2's
procedure, compared against a model given confidence alone.

| Model | AUROC | vs confidence | 95% CI | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| + feature (linear) | 0.746 | -0.001 | -0.005 to +0.005 | no effect |
| + interaction term | 0.744 | -0.002 | -0.019 to +0.014 | no effect |

Neither the linear term nor the explicit interaction changes the
model measurably. The objection that study 2 could not represent a
product is answered directly here: handed the product explicitly,
the model still does not improve.

**The raw product hurts while the fitted interaction is merely
inert, and the difference between those two is informative.** The
raw product is an unweighted combination: it forces the geometry in
at full strength, so whatever noise Sc carries goes straight into
the ranking. A logistic model handed the same interaction can give
it a coefficient near zero, and does. Taken together: there is no
signal here for the model to find, and using the product as a
ranking score without fitting it actively costs performance.

### The side-chain restriction, which bounds all of the above

Shape complementarity is a property of the molecular surface, and the
molecular surface of a binder modelled as backbone plus C-beta is not
the surface the real molecule has.

| Binder side chains | n | mean Sc |
|---|---|---|
| False | 962 | 0.562 |
| True | 191 | 0.502 |

On the 191 designs whose binder carries real side chains
(14 targets, binder rate 16.8%),
confidence scores 0.588 and the product
0.585: -0.002, 95% CI
-0.129 to +0.116, no effect.

This subset is small and **not representative** -- its binder rate
differs from the full set, because model status is confounded with
outcome -- so it is the weaker test, not the cleaner one.

## Which pre-registered rule applies

docs/SPEC.md section 8.6 fixes the decision rules before the output is
seen, so that the result cannot be rationalised afterwards. The rule that
applies here is the third one: **cannot reproduce their result**, for the
one feature that could be tested. Its instruction is explicit --
*do not claim they are wrong* -- and the reasons are worth stating, not
just citing:

| Their study | This study |
|---|---|
| 3,766 designs | 1153 designs |
| 11.6% binder rate | 27.0% |
| many campaigns, non-standardised binding definitions | one campaign, one adjudication rubric, two CROs |
| geometry on complexes **they re-predicted** | geometry on the release's **design models** |
| full-atom models throughout | side chains on 191 of 1153 binder models |
| Rosetta's `sc` filter | an independent reimplementation reading ~0.05 low |

Three times the data beats one campaign, and a single failure to
reproduce is weaker evidence than their positive across many. What this
study does establish is narrower and still worth having: **on this
dataset, with this definition of shape complementarity, the product form
does not rescue geometry.** The objection that study 2 could not have
detected a multiplicative effect has been answered by direct test, so the
thirteen-metric null is stronger than it was, for geometry of this kind.

## What this does and does not settle

- The **shape-complementarity** half of their result was tested directly,
  in their functional form, by their headline measure and by ours.
- The **interface energy** half was not tested at all, and it is the half
  they report as strongest. Nothing here speaks to it.
- Sc here is computed on **design models**, not on independently predicted
  complexes. Their geometry came from complexes they re-predicted. That
  remains the most plausible single explanation of any disagreement and
  it needs GPU time to test.
- Most binder models lack side chains, so for most of this dataset Sc is
  computed on a surface the real molecule does not have.

A failure to reproduce one half of a published result on one campaign is
weaker evidence than their positive across many, and it is reported here
as **not reproduced on this dataset**, not as a correction to their work.
